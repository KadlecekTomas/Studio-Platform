# Studio Platform Glossary

This glossary defines recurring terms used across the Studio Platform operating system. A domain-specific document may add precision, but should not silently redefine these terms.

## Accountability and roles

**Accountable owner** — the single person answerable for an outcome and for ensuring required decisions occur. Work may be delegated; accountability may not be vague or shared by default.

**Contributor** — a person who performs part of the work without owning the final outcome.

**Reviewer** — a person who independently assesses quality, evidence or risk. A reviewer must not approve work solely because they helped create it.

**Approver** — a person with explicit authority to accept a decision, commitment or residual risk.

**Service owner** — the person accountable for the ongoing business service, commitments, communication and lifecycle after launch.

**Technical owner** — the person accountable for technical integrity, maintainability and technical escalation of a system or component.

## Work and scope

**Scope** — the explicitly agreed set of outcomes, behaviours, deliverables, constraints and exclusions.

**Assumption** — a statement treated as true for planning but not yet established by sufficient evidence.

**Dependency** — something outside the immediate work owner’s control that must occur for an outcome to be achieved.

**Change request** — a proposed change to approved scope, acceptance, timing, cost, risk or responsibility that requires classification and decision.

**Defect** — implemented behaviour that fails to meet an approved requirement or acceptance criterion.

**Support request** — a request to explain, diagnose, restore or operate existing approved behaviour.

**New scope** — requested value not included in the approved commitment, even when it appears small or is described as a fix.

**Known limitation** — an explicit, accepted boundary of behaviour that is not currently treated as a defect.

## Decisions and gates

**Decision record** — a durable record of a consequential choice, including context, options, rationale, owner and consequences.

**Gate** — a formal decision point that requires defined evidence before work may proceed.

**Gate owner** — the person accountable for ensuring a gate decision is made; not necessarily the sole approver.

**Blocking condition** — an unresolved fact, risk, obligation or missing evidence that prevents progression.

**Exception** — an explicit, approved departure from a rule, with rationale, owner, residual risk and review or expiry condition.

**Residual risk** — risk remaining after current controls are applied and before any further mitigation.

## Evidence and knowledge

**Claim** — a statement presented as true and important enough to influence a decision, commitment or public representation.

**Evidence** — information that supports or contradicts a claim and identifies enough context to judge relevance and validity.

**Evidence record** — a durable link between a claim and its method, version, environment, result, owner and limitations.

**Source of truth** — the designated durable record used when information conflicts. Chat history and memory are not default sources of truth.

**Knowledge record** — a maintained operational fact, rule, lesson or method with ownership and lifecycle state.

**Project harvest** — the process of converting project outcomes, decisions and lessons into reusable capability and changed durable assets.

**Anecdote** — an individual observation that may motivate investigation but does not by itself establish a measured general outcome.

**Estimate** — a forecast or approximation based on stated assumptions, not a measured result.

## Delivery and quality

**Acceptance criterion** — an agreed, testable condition used to decide whether a deliverable or behaviour is accepted.

**Definition of Done** — the cross-functional minimum conditions that must hold before work is represented as complete.

**Release candidate** — a specific version proposed for launch after verification; it is not automatically approved for release.

**Release readiness** — the evidence-backed state in which product, data, operational, security, legal and recovery controls support a launch decision.

**Rollback** — a controlled return to a known prior state after an unsuccessful change.

**Recovery** — restoration of acceptable service and data integrity, which may not require returning to the exact prior version.

**Restoration** — return of usable service, potentially through a temporary mitigation.

**Permanent resolution** — removal or acceptable control of the underlying cause, not merely restoration.

**Finding** — an observed gap, risk or nonconformity requiring classification and disposition.

## Design

**Visual direction** — the deliberate system of composition, typography, colour, imagery, motion and tone that gives a product coherent character.

**Signature move** — one maintainable, product-relevant visual or interaction idea that contributes recognisability without reducing usability.

**Design acceptance** — explicit assessment that the current product version meets relevant visual, interaction, responsive and accessibility expectations.

**Polish pass** — a dedicated review and refinement stage after core behaviour exists; not a substitute for designing critical states earlier.

## Security, privacy and legal

**High-risk change** — a privileged, destructive, irreversible, security-sensitive, data-sensitive or operationally critical change requiring additional control.

**Personal data** — information relating to an identified or identifiable person, interpreted under applicable law and project context.

**Production data** — data originating from or materially representing a live operational environment, regardless of whether all fields are personal.

**Retention** — the controlled period for which data or records remain stored for a defined purpose.

**Deletion** — removal according to the applicable obligation and technical capability; disabling access alone may not constitute deletion.

**Intellectual property (IP)** — protectable work, rights, methods, code, design, content or know-how, subject to applicable agreements and law.

**Third party** — an external provider, library, platform, subcontractor or service on which delivery or operation depends.

**Subprocessor** — a third party processing personal data on behalf of a processor, where applicable.

## Commercial and service operations

**Commercial baseline** — the approved scope, price, cost assumptions, margin, payment plan and material dependencies against which project performance is assessed.

**Service level** — an explicit commitment about service response, communication, restoration or availability. Internal targets are not automatically client commitments.

**Response time** — time until a support case is acknowledged and ownership or next action is established.

**Priority** — the order of attention based on impact, urgency and reach, not on pressure, job title or message volume.

**Severity** — classification of the actual or potential consequence of a finding or incident. Severity is distinct from work priority.

**Support case** — the durable record of a support request, evidence, classification, communication, action and closure.

**Controlled service exit** — planned transfer, retirement or termination of support with ownership, access, data, documentation and obligations reconciled.

## Documentation states

**Draft** — not yet an approved operating rule.

**Active** — approved and applicable.

**Under review** — currently applicable while reassessment is underway.

**Superseded** — replaced by a named document or decision.

**Retired** — intentionally no longer applicable.

**Archived** — retained as historical evidence and not current guidance.
