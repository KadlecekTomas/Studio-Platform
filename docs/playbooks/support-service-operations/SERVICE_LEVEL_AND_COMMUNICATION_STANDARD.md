# Service Level and Communication Standard

## Purpose

This standard defines realistic service commitments, response behaviour and client communication during support and maintenance work.

## Principle

A service level is a controlled operating commitment, not a vague promise to be always available.

## Service definition

Before ongoing support begins, the service agreement must identify:

- supported products and environments,
- support hours and timezone,
- approved intake channels,
- priority definitions,
- response targets,
- restoration or resolution targets where explicitly agreed,
- excluded work,
- client responsibilities,
- escalation contacts,
- maintenance windows,
- pricing and consumption model,
- reporting cadence,
- termination and handover conditions.

## Response versus resolution

- **Response target** means acknowledgement, initial classification and ownership.
- **Restoration target** means return of an acceptable operational capability, possibly through a workaround.
- **Resolution target** means a verified permanent outcome.

These terms must not be presented as interchangeable.

## Communication rules

Every material update should state:

- what is confirmed,
- what remains unknown,
- current impact,
- action in progress,
- workaround or containment status,
- owner,
- next update time.

Do not provide false precision or speculate to reassure the client.

## Update cadence

The cadence is determined by severity and agreement. For active critical issues, updates continue even when there is no new resolution, because confirmation of continued ownership is itself operationally useful.

A missed communication target must be acknowledged and reviewed; it must not be hidden by retroactive timestamps.

## Pausing service clocks

A service clock may pause only when the agreement permits it and the record shows:

- the exact dependency,
- who must act,
- when the request was made,
- what work can continue meanwhile,
- when the item will be reviewed again.

Waiting for the client does not remove Studio Platform's obligation to manage and communicate the dependency.

## Client-caused urgency

Late client input, delayed approvals or avoidable deadline compression do not automatically create an emergency service obligation. Any exceptional commitment requires explicit owner approval and commercial classification.

## Escalation

Escalation is required when:

- impact exceeds the current priority,
- response or restoration targets are at risk,
- legal, security, privacy or financial exposure appears,
- ownership is unclear,
- the same failure repeats,
- client confidence is materially deteriorating.

## Service reporting

Periodic service reporting should cover:

- request volume by class and priority,
- response and restoration performance,
- recurring causes,
- ageing items,
- maintenance risk,
- client dependencies,
- commercial consumption,
- recommended preventive actions.

Metrics must be based on durable records, not reconstructed from memory.

## Prohibited commitments

Unless explicitly contracted and operationally funded, Studio Platform must not imply:

- 24/7 availability,
- guaranteed resolution times,
- unlimited support,
- immediate access to a specific founder or engineer,
- support for undocumented environments,
- free implementation of new scope.
