# Onboarding and Offboarding Standard

## Purpose

Ensure people gain the right context, access and support quickly, and leave without creating security, delivery or knowledge risks.

## Onboarding principles

Onboarding is complete only when the person can perform the role safely and independently. Account creation alone is not onboarding.

## Before start

The accountable manager must confirm:

- role purpose and expected outcomes;
- first 30-day priorities;
- named onboarding owner;
- required tools and access;
- confidentiality and contractual obligations;
- relevant playbooks, standards and client constraints;
- initial meetings and shadowing plan.

Access must follow least privilege and must not be granted without an identified business need.

## First-day minimum

The person receives:

- operating model and company principles;
- role and decision boundaries;
- communication channels and source-of-truth locations;
- security, privacy and incident expectations;
- current project context;
- escalation contacts;
- explanation of how work is reviewed and accepted.

## Capability verification

For roles affecting clients, production, finance, security or privileged systems, onboarding must include practical verification such as:

- supervised task;
- sandbox exercise;
- review of a representative change;
- incident or rollback walkthrough;
- evidence that required playbooks were understood.

## 30-day checkpoint

The manager records:

- outcomes achieved;
- unresolved access or context gaps;
- observed capability strengths;
- risks or support needs;
- next capability milestone.

## Offboarding trigger

Offboarding begins as soon as a departure, contract end, extended access suspension or role transfer is confirmed.

## Offboarding controls

The accountable owner must coordinate:

- inventory of accounts, devices, credentials and client systems;
- access revocation or transfer at the correct time;
- secret and key rotation where exposure existed;
- ownership transfer for repositories, domains, billing, vendors and documentation;
- open work, decisions, risks and commitments;
- client communication where necessary;
- return or deletion of company and client data;
- confirmation that personal copies are not retained.

## Knowledge handover

Critical knowledge must be transferred through durable records, not only meetings. The handover includes:

- current state;
- known risks and unresolved decisions;
- operational procedures;
- recurring obligations;
- access recovery paths;
- important relationships and commitments.

## Completion evidence

Offboarding is complete only when access, ownership, data and knowledge controls are verified by someone other than the departing person.

## Prohibited shortcuts

Do not:

- keep dormant access “just in case”;
- share another person’s account;
- leave production ownership unassigned;
- rely on chat history as the only handover;
- postpone revocation because documentation is incomplete.
