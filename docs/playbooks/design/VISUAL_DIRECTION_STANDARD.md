# Visual Direction Standard

## Purpose

This standard converts brand and product intent into an explicit visual direction before detailed UI production begins.

## Required visual direction brief

Before a major redesign, new product or public-facing experience, document:

- audience and context,
- product promise,
- three to five character attributes,
- emotional outcome,
- visual references and why they are relevant,
- anti-references and what must be avoided,
- typography strategy,
- colour logic,
- imagery or illustration approach,
- layout and spatial character,
- motion principle,
- accessibility constraints,
- one proposed signature move.

A moodboard alone is not a direction. Every reference must be translated into a design rule.

## Typography

Typography must establish hierarchy and personality while remaining readable.

Define:

- display and interface type roles,
- scale and line-height,
- maximum text widths,
- weight usage,
- numeric and tabular requirements,
- Czech diacritics and language coverage,
- fallback behaviour.

Do not use more type styles than the hierarchy requires. Avoid using weight, colour and size simultaneously to communicate the same distinction.

## Colour

Colour must have a system and a purpose.

Define:

- neutral foundation,
- primary brand expression,
- semantic colours,
- interactive states,
- surface hierarchy,
- dark-mode behaviour when applicable,
- contrast evidence.

Do not use brand colour for every emphasis. Semantic meaning must remain stable across the product.

## Layout and spacing

Define a spacing scale, content widths, grid behaviour and density modes. Composition should use intentional asymmetry or rhythm where appropriate, but workflow alignment must remain predictable.

## Shape and surface

Radius, borders, shadows and elevation must communicate structure. They are not decorative defaults. Repeated containers must justify their existence.

## Iconography and imagery

Use one coherent icon family or a documented custom approach. Images, screenshots and illustrations must support the product narrative and match the visual character.

Avoid random stock photography, mixed icon weights and AI imagery with inconsistent art direction.

## Motion

Motion must explain causality, continuity, hierarchy or feedback. Define standard durations, easing, reduced-motion behaviour and prohibited decorative patterns.

## Signature move test

The proposed signature move must be:

- relevant to the product story,
- repeatable across key surfaces,
- restrained enough not to become noise,
- technically maintainable,
- accessible,
- recognisable without relying only on a logo.

## Approval gate

Detailed design may proceed when:

- the character is explicit,
- references and anti-references are documented,
- typography and colour direction are viable,
- the signature move is defined,
- accessibility risks are identified,
- the design owner approves the direction.

If these conditions are not met, the work remains exploration rather than implementation-ready design.
