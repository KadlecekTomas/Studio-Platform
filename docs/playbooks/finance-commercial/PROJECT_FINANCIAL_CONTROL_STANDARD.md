# Project Financial Control Standard

## Purpose

Protect project margin, cashflow and commercial clarity from proposal through closure.

## Required financial baseline

Before delivery starts, every project must have:

- approved commercial model;
- contracted price or billing mechanism;
- invoicing milestones and payment terms;
- delivery cost baseline;
- target gross margin;
- named commercial owner;
- explicit assumptions and exclusions;
- treatment of third-party costs, taxes and currency exposure.

A project without this baseline is not financially ready.

## Source of truth

One controlled project financial record must contain budget, forecast, actuals, invoices, cash received and approved commercial changes. Private spreadsheets and chat estimates are not authoritative.

## Forecast discipline

The commercial owner reviews at least monthly:

- contracted revenue;
- recognized and forecast revenue;
- invoiced and collected cash;
- internal and external delivery cost;
- estimate to complete;
- forecast gross margin;
- overdue receivables;
- unapproved work and commercial risks.

Forecasts must represent the current expected outcome, not the original plan.

## Variance thresholds

Material variance includes any of the following:

- forecast cost exceeds baseline by 10% or more;
- forecast margin falls below the approved floor;
- invoice milestone is likely to slip;
- receivable becomes overdue;
- unapproved work exceeds one working day;
- external cost appears without budget coverage.

Material variance requires an owner, corrective action and decision date.

## Margin protection

Delivery teams must not absorb new scope silently. Rework caused by an internal defect is tracked separately from client-driven scope expansion. Discounts, write-offs and unpaid work require explicit approval.

## Financial closure

A project is financially closed only when:

- final acceptance and billable milestones are confirmed;
- all invoices are issued;
- receivables and disputed amounts are assigned;
- supplier costs are captured;
- final margin is calculated;
- remaining commercial obligations are documented;
- financial lessons are recorded.
