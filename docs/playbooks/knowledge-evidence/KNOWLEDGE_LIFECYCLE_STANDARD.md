# Knowledge Lifecycle Standard

## Purpose

Studio Platform treats knowledge as an operational asset. Important knowledge must be captured, owned, reviewed, discoverable and retired when it becomes stale.

## Knowledge classes

- **Decision knowledge** — why a material choice was made.
- **Delivery knowledge** — how a recurring activity is performed safely.
- **Product knowledge** — user, domain and workflow understanding.
- **Technical knowledge** — architecture, constraints, interfaces and failure modes.
- **Commercial knowledge** — pricing, scope, objections and deal lessons.
- **Evidence** — artifacts that support a claim, acceptance or control.

## Lifecycle

Every durable knowledge item moves through:

1. capture,
2. validation,
3. publication,
4. use,
5. review,
6. revision or retirement.

Chat messages, meeting notes and AI conversations are temporary capture surfaces, not permanent sources of truth.

## Required metadata

Every material knowledge item must identify:

- title and purpose,
- accountable owner,
- status: draft, active, superseded or retired,
- source or evidence,
- date created,
- last reviewed date,
- next review trigger,
- related systems, projects or decisions.

## Ownership

A document without an owner is unmaintained by definition. The owner is responsible for correctness, review and retirement, not necessarily for writing every update.

## Review triggers

Review is required when:

- a related system or process changes,
- an incident contradicts the document,
- a new project exposes missing guidance,
- regulation, vendor behaviour or architecture changes,
- the scheduled review date arrives.

## Stale knowledge

Stale content must not remain silently active. It must be updated, explicitly marked as uncertain, superseded or retired.

## Quality test

Knowledge is operational only when another competent person can find it, understand its context and use it without relying on the original author.
